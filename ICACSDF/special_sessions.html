Special Session

IoT & Cyber-Physical Security: Forensics and Threat Mitigation

IoT Security Cyber-Physical Systems Digital Forensics

Overview

This special session focuses on addressing the complex and unprecedented security and forensic challenges presented by the proliferation of IoT and Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS). The primary objective is to bring together researchers, industry practitioners, and academicians to explore state-of-the-art forensic frameworks, threat models, lightweight cryptography, and malware analysis specifically tailored for these connected environments.

Traditional cybersecurity protocols and forensic data acquisition methods often fail when applied to resource-constrained IoT devices and complex industrial control systems. This session will technically explore lightweight cryptographic frameworks, hardware-level security, edge-computing vulnerabilities, and novel digital forensic methodologies required to extract and preserve evidence from smart devices, SCADA systems, and interconnected sensor networks.

Topics of Interest

Digital Forensic Frameworks for Smart Home & Wearable IoT Devices Threat Modeling & Vulnerability Analysis in CPS and Industrial Control Systems Lightweight Cryptography & Secure Communication Protocols for Edge Computing Incident Response & Malware Analysis for IoT Botnets Data Privacy, Legal Frameworks & Chain-of-Custody Challenges in IoT Forensics

AI, Cyber Risk & Public Systems

AI Governance Cyber Risk Public Systems

Overview

Public systems encompassing district administration, judiciary, healthcare, energy grids, and public distribution are undergoing rapid AI-driven transformation, yet remain exposed to threats that conventional cybersecurity and digital forensics frameworks were never designed to anticipate. As algorithmic decision-making embeds itself across every layer of governance, the consequences of failure escalate beyond technical disruption — adversarial manipulation, data poisoning, opaque audit trails, and agentic AI failures now threaten not just operational continuity, but the integrity of democratic institutions themselves. The challenge is not merely securing AI; it is ensuring that AI deployed in public life remains transparent, forensically auditable, and answerable to the citizens it serves.

This session convenes researchers, academics, and industry professionals at this critical intersection, advancing rigorous, interdisciplinary frameworks for cybersecurity resilience, digital forensics accountability, and ethical AI governance across the systems that underpin modern public life.
